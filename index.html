Welcome to Taniti

Taniti is a small, tropical island in the Pacific. While the island has an area of less than 500 square miles, the terrain is varied and includes both sandy and rocky beaches, a small but safe harbor, lush tropical rainforests, and a mountainous interior that includes a small, active volcano. Taniti has an indigenous population of about 20,000. Until a recent increase in tourism, most of the Tanitian economy was dominated by fishing or agriculture.

Activities

Most people visit Taniti to enjoy the beaches, explore the rainforest, and visit the volcano. You can also go snorkeling, ziplining, or on a chartered fishing tour, and visit museums, galleries, and nightlife spots.

Lodging

Restaurants

Taniti has 10 restaurants: five serve mostly local fish and rice, three serve American-style meals, and two serve Pan-Asian cuisine. Two supermarkets, two smaller grocery stores, and a 24-hour convenience store are also available.

Transportation

Most visitors arrive by air, and a small cruise ship docks in Yellow Leaf Bay one night per week. On the island, you can use public or private buses, taxis, rental cars, or bikes, or explore on foot.

FAQ

What kind of power outlets does Taniti use?

Power outlets are 120 volts, the same as in the United States.

What is the currency?

Taniti uses the U.S. dollar, but many businesses also accept euros and yen. Several banks facilitate currency exchange, and many businesses accept major credit cards.

What are the alcohol rules?

Alcohol is not allowed to be served or sold between midnight and 9:00 a.m. The drinking age is 18 and is not strictly enforced.

Will people speak English?

Many younger Tanitians speak fluent English. Very little English is spoken in rural areas, especially by older residents.

Is there medical care?

There is one hospital and several clinics. The hospital has many multilingual employees.

Is Taniti safe?

Violent crime is very rare in Taniti, but as tourism increases there are more reports of pickpocketing and other petty crimes.

Are places closed on holidays?

Taniti enjoys many national holidays, and many tourist attractions and restaurants are closed on holidays, so plan accordingly.

Activities

Entertainment: Most people visit Taniti to enjoy the beaches, explore the rainforest, and visit the volcano. Other things to do include a local history museum, chartered fishing tours, snorkeling, ziplining in the rainforest, several pubs (including a microbrewery), a new dance club, a movie theater, helicopter rides, an arcade, art galleries, and bowling. A nine-hole golf course should be operational by next year. Many of these activities are in Merriton Landing, a rapidly developing area on the north side of Yellow Leaf Bay.

Sightseeing: Most tourists spend most of their time in Taniti City, which boasts native architecture and nearby white, sandy beaches that encircle Yellow Leaf Bay.

Other popular activities:

  • Boat or bus tours of the island
  • Hikes in the rainforest
  • Visits to Taniti’s active volcano

Transportation

Getting to Taniti: Almost all visitors arrive in Taniti by air, though some arrive on a small cruise ship that docks in Yellow Leaf Bay for one night per week. Taniti is served by a small airport that can accommodate small jets and propeller planes. The airport is being expanded so larger jets will be able to land on the island within the next few years.
